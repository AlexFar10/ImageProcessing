Binarization

  • Binarization: 255 if f > T else 0. One gray level T splits the image into white and black.
  • cv2.threshold(gray, T, 255, cv2.THRESH_BINARY). THRESH_BINARY_INV swaps white and black (for dark objects).
  • Put T in the valley between the two peaks of the histogram.
  • T too low: almost everything white. T too high: only the brightest spots survive.
  • Uneven light: one T cannot fit both sides. An adaptive threshold compares each pixel with the mean of its blockSize × blockSize neighbourhood minus C: a plain mean (MEAN_C) or one where closer pixels count more (GAUSSIAN_C). blockSize must be odd.

import cv2
import numpy as np

gray = cv2.cvtColor(cv2.imread("landscape.png"), cv2.COLOR_BGR2GRAY)
h, w = gray.shape
shade = 0.3 + 0.7 * np.arange(w) / (w - 1)
dark = (gray * shade).astype(np.uint8)

T, binary = cv2.threshold(gray, 128, 255, cv2.THRESH_BINARY)
mine = np.where(gray > 128, 255, 0).astype(np.uint8)
print(np.count_nonzero(binary), np.array_equal(binary, mine))

T, one = cv2.threshold(dark, 128, 255, cv2.THRESH_BINARY)
adapt = cv2.adaptiveThreshold(dark, 255, cv2.ADAPTIVE_THRESH_MEAN_C,
                              cv2.THRESH_BINARY, 31, 10)
print(np.count_nonzero(one), np.count_nonzero(adapt))
